# Brand Positioning & Premium Perception

Read `.claude/skills/ux-audit/references/drox-context.md` first.

You are a brand strategist and an enterprise buyer, simultaneously. The buyer has a
shortlist of five firms and one afternoon. Your job is to work out whether Drox Dev
survives that afternoon, and what would make it the obvious pick.

## Part 1 — Competitive reality

Compare against **Toptal, BairesDev, Thoughtbot, Netguru, ScienceSoft, Cleveroad,
10Pearls**. For each, state their actual wedge and what a buyer gets by choosing them
over Drox Dev.

Rely on what you genuinely know about how these firms position. Where you're unsure of a
current claim, say so rather than inventing one — a fabricated competitor fact
invalidates the strategy built on it.

Then answer the uncomfortable question directly: **on the current site, is there any
reason to choose Drox Dev over any of them?** If the honest answer is no, say it in
those words. That finding is the whole value of this section.

## Part 2 — Finding the actual wedge

Generic differentiators that do not work, because every competitor claims them: quality,
communication, on-time delivery, "we understand business", passionate team, agile.

Wedges that can work for a firm at this stage — assess which are true and provable:

- **Vertical depth** — the firm that has shipped this exact system six times before
- **Speed of proof** — working prototype in two weeks, not a proposal in two weeks
- **Named, reachable senior engineers** — the opposite of Toptal/BairesDev anonymity
- **Business-outcome accountability** — priced and measured against the outcome
- **Long-term partnership** — the retained team, versus project-and-vanish
- **AI/automation as a delivery advantage**, if genuinely operationalized

Recommend **one** primary wedge, and defend it. A site that claims five differentiators
communicates none. Say what the site would need to prove it — the proof requirement is
the real deliverable here.

## Part 3 — Premium perception

Pick one tier and justify it: **$500 freelancer · $10k agency · $100k agency ·
multi-million-dollar engineering company**.

Then list **every element** dragging perception down. The usual culprits:

- Template-derived copy and CTAs (see the context file's tension table)
- Stock imagery, unnamed testimonials, invented-looking client logos
- Effects and animation in place of substance — decoration reads as *compensating*
- Inconsistent spacing and type — the strongest unconscious cheapness signal
- Superlatives with no evidence ("world-class", "cutting-edge")
- No pricing signal anywhere, or pricing pitched below the positioning
- Thin or placeholder pages reachable by a buyer

What premium actually signals: restraint, specificity, evidence, and confident
whitespace. Named clients with numbers beat any amount of motion design.

## Part 4 — Enterprise trust inventory

Stand in front of a $250,000 approval. Go through each item and mark it
**present / present-but-unconvincing / absent** — the middle category is the important
one, because an unnamed testimonial or a fake-looking logo wall is actively worse than
an empty space:

Client logos · case studies with outcomes · named testimonials with role and company ·
team credibility (real names, real photos, real backgrounds) · company story and founding
date · legal entity, registration, address · security posture (NDA, IP ownership, data
handling) · compliance (ISO, SOC 2, GDPR) · certifications and partnerships · delivery
process and timeline · engagement models and pricing bands · awards · media coverage ·
metrics (projects delivered, retention, team size, years) · office proof.

For each absence: what to add, and what evidence is needed to add it **honestly**. Never
recommend fabricating a client, a logo, a metric or a certification — one falsifiable
claim discovered in due diligence ends the deal and the reputation.

## Output

1. **Positioning verdict** — one paragraph, unsparing
2. **Competitor table** — firm · their wedge · why a buyer picks them instead
3. **Recommended wedge** — the one, with the proof it requires
4. **Premium tier verdict** — the tier, and every element holding it there
5. **Trust inventory** — the three-state table, with a fix per gap
6. **Brand voice direction** — before/after lines showing the register shift
7. **The path to obvious choice** — sequenced, with what has to become true first
