**Site:** https://www.droxdev.com · **Date:** 29 July 2026 · **Revision 2**

## Revision note — what the Company Profile changed

Revision 1 was written without the Company Profile, which was referenced in the brief but not supplied. Reading it changes the audit's central thesis and corrects one finding outright.

**One finding was wrong.** Rev 1 said the services section "contradicts your positioning" because it lists digital marketing, SEO and social media. It does not. The profile (p.14) lists **Digital Marketing** as one of nine formal service lines, with SEO, social media, Google & Meta ads, content strategy and performance marketing beneath it. That was my error — I inferred the offering from the shorter list in the brief. The *strategic* question of whether to lead with it stands, and is re-argued in §14 on its merits, but it is a judgement call, not a contradiction.

**Everything else got worse, in a specific and useful way.**

Rev 1's core recommendation was "create the missing trust content." That was wrong too — not because the content is missing, but because **it already exists and is excellent.** The profile contains, in finished form:

| Rev 1 said "Absent" | The profile has |
| --- | --- |
| No case studies | **Two**, fully structured — challenge, solution, features, stack, metrics (Alfa: 100+ events, 200+ staff, 5 modules; Droxlink: 3 modules, QR access) |
| No team credibility | **Four named founders** with titles, defined ownership areas, and a written accountability model |
| No company story | A written founding story and a stated founding observation |
| No engagement models | **Five**, tabulated with "best for" guidance |
| No delivery process | A **7-stage** engineering process |
| No industries served | 13, listed |
| No tech stack depth | Five categories, fully specified |
| No differentiation | A written "What Makes Us Different" and a "Why Drox Dev" section |

**So the real finding is not a content gap. It is a transmission failure.**

You have two artefacts describing the same company. One is a disciplined, business-first, enterprise-credible document written for a buyer. The other is a template-derived website written for a developer. **The profile is the site that should exist.** Almost every Priority-2 item in §20 changes from *authoring* to *transcription* — which makes it dramatically cheaper and moves the realistic timeline for enterprise readiness from ~6 weeks to ~2.

The profile also validates the wedge I recommended in §14 before I had read it. p.24 already says the four founders "stay close to the work" and that "the person setting that direction stays involved in the engagement rather than handing it on." That is precisely the positioning I argued for — already articulated, and completely absent from droxdev.com.

**Two new findings the profile itself introduces**, both in §11 and §22:

1. **The "2" vs "26+" contradiction.** p.5 states **2 projects delivered**. p.22 states **26+ projects delivered**. Both are true under different definitions — 2 company projects, 26+ across the founders' prior careers — but they sit 17 pages apart with no reconciling label, and the website reproduces the larger number without the qualifier. Any buyer who reads both will treat it as inflation, and they will be right to ask.
2. **The vision statement problem is worse, not better.** "To become the world's most trusted technology partner" appears in the profile (p.8) as well as on the site. Rev 1 flagged it as a website copy issue. It is a positioning issue, and it is the single line in an otherwise disciplined document that reads as unearned.

Sections revised below: **1, 3, 9, 11, 14, 17, 19, 20, 21, 22.** Sections 2, 4–8, 10, 12, 13, 15, 16, 18 are unchanged — the profile does not touch the hero, the funnel, the technical state, or the visual system.

## 1. Executive Summary

### Overall impression

This is a **well-crafted front-end wrapped around a business that has not been explained, proved, or made contactable** — while a document that does all three sits finished on your hard drive.

Someone spent real effort on the visual layer of this site: the section headers, card treatments, the black-hole hero, the accordion process section. Someone else — possibly the same people — wrote a genuinely enterprise-grade company profile with two structured case studies, four named founders, five engagement models and a seven-stage delivery process. **Almost none of the second thing appears in the first.**

The site does not currently function as a lead-generation asset. It functions as a demo reel — and it is a demo reel for a company that has already written the sales asset it needs.

### Biggest strengths

1. **Genuine visual craft in places.** The card hover systems, the section-header component, the process accordion on `/services` are above the level of a typical regional agency site.
2. **The Company Profile is excellent, and it is the strongest asset the business owns.** It is disciplined, specific, business-first, and written in a register the website never reaches. Compare "That commitment is easier to state than to sustain. It shows up in unglamorous places — a scope agreed honestly, an architecture that still holds as the business changes, a call returned long after the invoice is settled" (profile p.25) with "we promise we don't bite" (`components/FAQ.tsx:14`). Same company, two different firms.
3. **Two real, named, photographed testimonials** with a specific, believable problem→solution narrative (`components/Testimonials.tsx:16-31`). Both appear in the profile too — with the metrics the site omits.
4. **Content is centralized** in `config/content.ts`, so copy fixes are cheap to ship.
5. **Clean build.** TypeScript passes, Biome passes, all 8 routes prerender static.

### Biggest weaknesses

1. **The homepage hero has no call to action.** None. Not a weak one — zero (`components/Hero.tsx`). The `ctaButtons` array in `config/content.ts:56-68` (labelled "Documentation" and "GitHub") is dead config that no component reads.
2. **The contact form does not capture leads.** It opens WhatsApp (`app/contact/page.tsx:85-103`). Nothing is stored, emailed, or recorded. Close the tab and the enquiry is gone permanently.
3. **The site sells four of your nine service lines, and omits the two that carry the positioning.** The profile (p.14) defines nine: Software Engineering, Web Development, E-Commerce, Mobile, **AI Solutions**, **Business Automation**, UI/UX, Digital Marketing, Support. The site shows six — and **AI Solutions and Business Automation are not among them** (`components/ServiceCards.tsx:19-98`). The two capabilities that differentiate you from every web agency in Kerala are the two the website doesn't mention. *(Rev 1 wrongly called this a contradiction; digital marketing is a real service line. The omission is the finding.)*
4. **Seven broken or empty destinations** a visitor can reach: `/portfolio`, `/terms`, `/privacy`, `/accessibility` are 404s linked from the footer; `/blog`, `/docs`, `/pricing` are live pages rendering a single bare heading.
5. **Assets are catastrophically oversized.** `public/projects/alfa1.png` is **32 MB**. `public/projects/drox1.png` is 7.7 MB. `public/video/silk-1770108089707.webm` is **22 MB** and autoplays twice on `/about`.
6. **Every page shares one title and one meta description.** There is exactly one `metadata` export in the entire app (`app/layout.tsx:12`). All eight routes appear in Google as "DRO X — Building exceptional digital experiences and web solutions."
7. **The copy is written for a different buyer.** "where we played, experimented" (`components/Projects.tsx:37-41`); "we promise we don't bite" (`components/FAQ.tsx:14`); "FAQ It Up!"; "Wall of Love"; "Loved by Thinkers". A CFO approving spend reads this as a hobby project. Your own profile does not make a single one of these mistakes.
8. **The proof exists and is unpublished.** Two structured case studies with real metrics — Alfa (100+ events, 200+ staff, 5 modules, live at alfaeventsitebooking.cloud) and Droxlink — sit in the profile. The website shows two unlabelled screenshots with `alt="Project Preview"`.

### Overall recommendation

**Do not redesign. Do not write new content. Publish the content you already have.**

The visual layer is not the bottleneck — it is the only part of the *website* that is finished. And the content layer is not missing — it is finished too, in the wrong artefact. What's missing is the connection between them.

Roughly 80% of the business value available here comes from about two weeks of work that involves almost no design and almost no original writing:

- put a CTA in the hero, make the form capture leads
- **port the profile onto the site** — case studies, founders, engagement models, process, industries
- delete or build the empty pages, fix the four broken footer links
- compress the images, add per-page metadata
- rewrite roughly 40 lines of website copy to the register the profile already uses

Only after that does it make sense to argue about visual polish.

One thing cannot be fixed by porting, because the profile has it too. Drox Dev was founded **in 2026** — months ago. Four founders, one paying client, two delivered platforms. That is an honest, promising early-stage engineering company, and the profile mostly tells that story well. But two lines break it: the vision statement claims a path to being "the world's most trusted technology partner" (p.8), and the same document reports both "2 projects delivered" (p.5) and "26+ projects delivered" (p.22).

**A buyer will find that inconsistency, and it will cost you more than the numbers earn.** §14 argues you should stop reaching for the inflated story and sell the real one, because at your actual stage it converts better — and because your profile already proves you can write it.

## 2. First Impression (5-Second Test)

Reading the hero exactly as a stranger would:

> **Make Beautiful** (in animated colour) **Digital Experiences**
> Beautiful, fast and modern web solutions.
> [badge: "DROX - Your TECH Partner"]
> — then a black hole animation with 170 orbiting particles.

**What do you think this company does?**
A web design studio. Possibly a creative agency. Possibly a developer tool — the black hole and the terminal icon in the badge read as "open-source project". Nothing indicates custom software, AI, automation, or enterprise systems. The word "software" does not appear above the fold. Neither does any industry, any client type, or any business outcome.

**Does it feel premium?** Partly. The dark theme, the gradient type and the motion signal *effort*. But premium is signalled by restraint and evidence; this signals decoration. 5/10.

**Does it feel trustworthy?** No. There is no company name resolution ("DRO X" vs "DROX" vs "Drox Dev" — three spellings in the codebase), no client, no number, no person, no address above the fold.

**Does it look expensive?** It looks like an expensive *template*. Which is a specific and damaging impression: it suggests the money went into the theme, not the engineering.

**Does it feel enterprise-ready?** No. Enterprise-ready sites answer "who else trusts you" in the first screen. This one answers "look what we can animate."

**Would you stay?** A developer would. A CTO evaluating vendors would scroll once. A CEO would leave.

**Would you leave? Why?**
Because there is **nothing to click.** The hero has no button. The only action available in the first viewport is the "Chat Now" link in the navbar — and on mobile that link is `hidden md:inline-flex` (`components/navbar.tsx:98`), so on a phone the first screen contains **zero** ways to contact the company.

### Score: **3/10**

Points for craft. Everything else fails the only test that matters: a stranger cannot tell what you sell, to whom, or what to do next.

## 3. Target Audience Audit

### Who the site is actually speaking to

Reading the artefacts rather than the intent:

| Signal | Implied audience |
| --- | --- |
| "Documentation" / "GitHub" CTAs in config, terminal icon in badge, tech-logo carousel | **Developers** |
| "Make Beautiful Digital Experiences", "Design-Driven", "Beautiful interfaces" | **Design-conscious founders** |
| SEO, social media marketing, e-commerce, "one-stop-shop for all things digital" | **Local small businesses** |
| "we promise we don't bite", "FAQ It Up!", "Wall of Love" | **Very small businesses / first-time buyers** |
| WhatsApp as the primary and only working contact channel | **Local SME buyers in India** |
| Mission copy: "custom software, AI, automation… measurable, scalable, long-term value" (`app/about/page.tsx:112-117`) | **Enterprise / mid-market ops leaders** |

**Six different audiences.** The site is speaking to everyone, which means it converts no one with conviction.

The tell is that the *only* place the intended positioning appears verbatim is the About page mission statement — one paragraph, buried on the second-most-visited page, and lifted word-for-word from the profile (p.8). It is the single sentence that made the jump from the good document to the weak one.

The profile is clearer about the buyer than the site is, though not by as much as it should be. It names "startups, small and medium-sized businesses, and growing enterprises" (p.4) and then lists **13 industries** (p.23) — retail, e-commerce, education, healthcare, hospitality, real estate, construction, logistics, manufacturing, professional services, startups, SMEs, enterprise organisations. For a four-person firm with two delivered projects, 13 industries reads as *no* industry. That list is a liability in the profile and would be a worse one on the site.

### Who it should speak to

Given a 2026-founded, four-founder team in Kozhikode with a delivered event-operations platform: the realistic, winnable buyer is the **operations-led SME and mid-market decision-maker with a broken internal process** — a COO, an operations director, a founder of a 20–200 person business. Not the Fortune 500 CTO. Not the local restaurant wanting a website.

The Alfa Events testimonial is the proof of exactly this buyer: *"We struggled to manage catering staff across multiple events at the same time… they built a web app where we can list events and staff can view and book available jobs."* That is a business-automation case study, and it is the most on-strategy sentence on the entire website. It is currently sitting in a section called "Wall of Love."

### Improvements

1. **Add the two missing service lines.** AI Solutions and Business Automation are in the profile (p.14) with full sub-capability lists — AI assistants, workflow automation, document processing, LLM integration, CRM/HR/finance automation, approval workflows. Neither appears on the site. These are your differentiators; publish them.
2. **Demote Digital Marketing rather than delete it.** *(Revised.)* It is a genuine service line, so removing it would misrepresent the business. But leading with SEO and social media alongside custom software is what makes the site read as a small agency. Put the six engineering-led services first, and place Digital Marketing last, framed as a support capability for clients you're already building for — not as a standalone offer.
3. **Lead with the operations problem, not the deliverable.** "We build the software that runs your operations" beats "Web Development." The profile's own "Business Challenges We Solve" page (p.12) does exactly this — manual and repetitive processes, disconnected systems, no centralised data, poor workflow visibility. **That page should be a homepage section, close to verbatim.**
4. **Cut 13 industries to three.** On current evidence: **events & hospitality** (Alfa, delivered), **retail & professional services** (Droxlink's buyer), and one you're actively pursuing. Keep the full list in the profile if you like; do not put it on the site.
5. **Delete the developer signals**: the terminal icon in the badge (`components/chip.tsx:2,10`), the tech-logo carousel as a *hero-adjacent* section, the GitHub link in the primary contact grid.
6. **Choose one brand spelling.** The site uses "DRO X" (`config/site.ts:4` — every page title and the footer copyright) and "DROX" (`config/content.ts:12`). The profile uses "drox.dev", "Drox Dev" and "DROX DEV". That is **five** variants across two artefacts. Standardise: **Drox Dev** for titles, legal and prose; **DROX** for the wordmark; `droxdev.com` for the domain.

### Rewrite

Two directions. I recommend the first.

**Option A — Operations wedge (recommended)**

> ### Custom software that runs your operations.
>
> Drox Dev builds the systems small and mid-sized businesses run on — scheduling, logistics, inventory, internal tools — with AI and automation where it earns its keep. Not templates. Software built around how your business actually works.
>
> **[ Book a 30-minute scoping call ]**  **[ See how we rebuilt Alfa Events' staffing → ]**

Why: names the buyer, names the problem category, names the mechanism, and the secondary CTA routes to your single strongest proof asset instead of nowhere.

**Option B — Outcome-first, more conservative**

> ### Stop running your business on spreadsheets and WhatsApp groups.
>
> We build custom software, AI tools and automation for growing businesses — starting with a two-week working prototype, not a six-week proposal.
>
> **[ Book a free scoping call ]**  **[ View our work → ]**

Why: leads with a visceral, recognisable pain and immediately de-risks with a concrete delivery promise. Sharper, and the "two-week prototype" line is a genuine wedge against BairesDev and Toptal (§14).

**Sub-headline trust bar** — add immediately beneath the CTAs, before the animation. *(Revised with profile data.)*

> 100+ events and 200+ staff managed on software we built · Two platforms in production · Kozhikode, Kerala

Three facts, all verifiable, all from your own profile (pp.5, 19, 20), all currently absent from the first screen.

**Do not** use "26+ projects delivered" here. The profile reports 2 company projects on p.5 and 26+ on p.22, and the second figure is the founders' cumulative career total. Unqualified on a homepage, it is the claim a buyer checks first and the one you cannot defend. The Alfa metrics are stronger anyway — they describe *what the software does*, which is what the buyer is actually purchasing.

**Additional hero fixes**
- Replace the badge text "DROX - Your TECH Partner" (meaningless, and the ALL-CAPS "TECH" reads as amateur) with something factual: "Custom software · AI · Automation".
- Replace the terminal icon (`components/chip.tsx:2`) — it signals developer tooling.
- Fix the **two `<h1>` elements** at `components/Hero.tsx:27` and `:32`. One page, one `<h1>`.
- Keep the black hole, but shrink it so the fold contains headline + subhead + CTA + trust bar. The animation should be the reward for scrolling, not the substitute for content.

## 6. Information Architecture

### Current homepage flow (`app/page.tsx`)

| # | Section | What the buyer learns | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Hero + black hole | Nothing about the business | Decorative |
| 2 | Why Choose Us — 26+ / 9+ / 50+ counters | Three numbers with no context | Premature |
| 3 | About / Benefits — 6 generic benefit tiles | Nothing specific | Filler |
| 4 | Service Cards — 6 services | What you sell (wrong list) | **First useful section, ~3 screens down** |
| 5 | Tech Stack carousel | React, Next, TS, Tailwind, Node, Mongo, Python, Figma | Irrelevant to the buyer |
| 6 | Projects — 2 unlabelled screenshots | Nothing (no titles, no outcomes) | Wasted |
| 7 | Testimonials — 2 quotes, one client | **The strongest asset on the site** | Buried at ~85% scroll |
| 8 | FAQ | Off-register, wrong questions | Damaging |
| — | *(no closing CTA)* | The page simply ends | **Critical gap** |

**Does it tell a story?** No. It is stacked sections in roughly reverse order of importance. The proof (§7) is at the bottom; the numbers (§2) are at the top with nothing yet to make them meaningful. A visitor learns what you actually sell three screens in, and reaches the end of the page with no action offered.

Two structural facts make this worse:
- **The page has no closing CTA.** `app/page.tsx` ends with `<FAQ />`. Everyone who reads the whole page — your most engaged visitors — hits nothing.
- **Section spacing is arbitrary**: `md:mt-[350px]`, `mt-[100px]`, `mt-[150px]`, `mt-[-50px]`, `mt-[-20px]` (`app/page.tsx:24-44`). Rhythm by hand-tuning.

### Proposed homepage flow

| # | Section | Job | Question it answers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Hero** — outcome headline, dual CTA, trust bar | Orient + capture | "What is this and what do I do?" |
| 2 | **Problem** — 3 recognisable operational pains | Resonate | "Do they understand my situation?" |
| 3 | **Services** — 6 cards, correct list, business-outcome framing | Qualify | "Do they do what I need?" |
| 4 | **Featured case study — Alfa Events**, expanded to a full narrative with the metric | **Prove** | "Have they done this before?" |
| 5 | **How we work** — Discovery → Prototype → Build → Launch → Support, with timelines | De-risk | "What happens if I engage?" |
| 6 | **Team** — four founders, named, photographed, with backgrounds | Humanise | "Who is actually accountable?" |
| 7 | **Numbers** — 26+ / 9+ / 50+, *now* meaningful | Reinforce | "What's their track record?" |
| 8 | **Testimonials** | Reinforce | "Does anyone vouch for them?" |
| 9 | **FAQ** — rewritten to the deal-blocking questions | Remove objections | "What's the catch?" |
| 10 | **Closing CTA** — booking widget, response-time promise, phone, email | **Convert** | "How do I start?" |
| — | *(Tech stack moves to `/services` or the footer)* | — | — |

The principle: **every section answers the question the previous one raised.** Numbers before proof are noise; numbers after a case study are corroboration. That reordering is roughly one hour of work in `app/page.tsx` and is among the cheapest high-impact changes available.

### What the benchmark sites actually do

Be specific about the mechanism, because the gap is mechanical, not magical:

**Linear** ships roughly four type sizes and one accent colour across its entire marketing site. This site ships four `tv()` sizes plus at least seven arbitrary pixel sizes plus dozens of Tailwind presets, and 46 hex colours. That difference alone accounts for most of the perceived polish gap — it is not a talent gap, it is a **decision** gap.

**Stripe** puts a customer logo wall above the fold and a named case study within one scroll. Its hero animation is subordinate to the value proposition, never a substitute for it. This site's hero animation *is* the content.

**Vercel** and **Supabase** run one icon set, one radius scale, one shadow ladder. Every surface's visual altitude is deliberate.

**Cursor** and **Framer** both use heavy motion — but always demonstrating the product. Their animations *are* proof. A black hole is not proof of software engineering; it is proof that you can use a WebM file.

**The pattern worth naming:** every one of those seven sites is more **restrained** than this one. Their premium signal comes from consistency and confident whitespace, not from effects. A site running more animation libraries than it has design tokens reads as *less* expensive, not more — because effects are cheap and consistency is not, and buyers unconsciously know that.

### Design-system fixes, ranked

1. **Consolidate to one icon library.** Lucide (already in 10 files) or Phosphor (4 files). Removing the other three cuts dependencies and fixes the most visible inconsistency. `@tabler/icons-react` is used in 3 files and is the easiest to remove first.
2. **Make the tokens authoritative.** Set `--primary` to the violet brand colour, define `--reflect-sub` in `:root` as well as `.dark`, then replace the 90 hardcoded hex occurrences with token references. Do this file-by-file, starting with `ServiceCards`, `about-section` and `Footer` (the biggest offenders).
3. **One radius scale.** Pick three values (`--radius-sm/md/lg`), map the current five to them.
4. **One button component.** Extend `CTAButton` with `variant` (primary/secondary/ghost) and `size`, then replace the four bespoke button implementations.
5. **One card component** with variants, replacing the four treatments.
6. **Delete `gsap` and `@gsap/react`** — they are imported by **zero** files. Delete `framer-motion`; it is imported by exactly one file (`navbar.tsx`) while `motion/react` serves the other 16. These are free wins.
7. **Standardise motion.** Define shared duration and easing constants. Currently: 0.4s, 0.5s, 0.6s, 0.7s, 0.8s, 1.0s and a 1.2s Lenis duration all coexist with different easings.

That is not a content problem or a design problem. It is a **distribution** problem, and distribution problems are the cheapest kind to fix.

Three things, in order:

1. **Fix the funnel** — hero CTA, a form that captures leads, working links. Two days. Without this, nothing else compounds.
2. **Publish the profile.** Four days. It flips two enterprise gates, triples your Trust score and doubles your Copywriting score using content that is already written and already approved.
3. **Then** argue about the black hole.
